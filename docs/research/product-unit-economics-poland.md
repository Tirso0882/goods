# Product unit economics in Poland: shipping, returns and payment fees by category

Research date: 28 September 2026. Sources are primary only: the carriers' own price lists and rules (InPost, ORLEN Paczka, DPD Polska, Poczta Polska/Pocztex), Stripe's Polish pricing pages, the consolidated text of the Polish consumer rights act from the Sejm's legal database, and named studies for return rates (EEA/ETC CE, the European Commission's JRC, Alsendo with SW Research, Global24). Every price below was read from the owner's page or PDF on the research date.

**Short version:**

- The cheapest public option for most small parcels is ORLEN Paczka (13.49 to 15.49 PLN gross, any weight up to 20 kg). DPD Pickup is cheaper for very light parcels (12.99 PLN up to 0.5 kg). InPost XS is cheapest of all (11.49 PLN) but only for items up to 4 cm thick and 3 kg, sent from the InPost Mobile app.
- InPost's business locker list is more expensive for a small seller than its retail list once the 13% fuel surcharge and VAT are added.
- Stripe charges 1.5% + 1.00 PLN for standard EEA cards, 1.6% + 1.00 PLN for BLIK and 1.9% + 1.00 PLN for Przelewy24. Stripe does not give the fee back when you refund.
- Under Polish law the consumer pays the direct cost of sending a return back, but only if you told them before the purchase. If you did not, you pay. You must always refund the original delivery cost, up to the cheapest standard option you offered.
- Sourced return rates exist only for a few categories: clothing (16% in Poland, 20% EU), footwear (30% EU), electronics (about 5% EU), beauty (2%, cross-border from Poland). Most other categories have no published figure.

## Consumer law on returns

The current consolidated text is the [Obwieszczenie Marszałka Sejmu of 4 September 2026, Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1244](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/1244/text.pdf), published on 23 September 2026. It is listed as the latest consolidated text of the [ustawa z dnia 30 maja 2014 r. o prawach konsumenta](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU20140000827). The ISAP site shows a captcha to automated tools, so I read the same document through the Sejm's official ELI API, which gives the same text.

- **Art. 27 ust. 1.** A consumer who bought at a distance can withdraw within 14 days, without giving a reason and without costs, except the costs in art. 33, art. 34 ust. 2 and art. 35.
- **Art. 28 pkt 1.** For goods, the 14 days start when the consumer (or a person they name, other than the carrier) takes possession of the goods.
- **Art. 29 ust. 1.** If you did not tell the consumer about the right to withdraw, the right runs for an extra 12 months.
- **Art. 32 ust. 1.** You must refund all payments, including the cost of delivering the goods, within 14 days of receiving the withdrawal notice. Under **art. 32 ust. 3** you can hold the refund until you get the goods back or receive proof they were sent, whichever comes first, unless you offered to collect them yourself.
- **Art. 33.** If the consumer chose a delivery method more expensive than the cheapest usual one you offered, you do not have to refund the extra cost. In practice you refund delivery up to your cheapest standard option.
- **Art. 34 ust. 1.** The consumer must send the goods back within 14 days of withdrawing.
- **Art. 34 ust. 2.** The consumer pays only the direct cost of returning the goods, unless you agreed to pay it or did not tell them they have to pay it.
- **Art. 34 ust. 4.** The consumer is liable for any loss in value caused by using the goods beyond what is needed to check their nature, features and how they work.
- **Art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 10.** Before the contract you must tell the consumer what return costs they will pay. For goods that cannot be sent back by normal post because of their nature, you must tell them the cost of returning them. This matters for furniture.
- **Art. 38 ust. 1.** There is no right of withdrawal for, among others:
  - pkt 3: goods made to the consumer's specification or for their individual needs;
  - pkt 4: goods that spoil quickly or have a short shelf life;
  - pkt 5: goods in a sealed package that cannot be returned for health or hygiene reasons, if the package was opened after delivery;
  - pkt 6: goods that, by their nature, become inseparably mixed with other goods after delivery;
  - pkt 9: sound or video recordings or software in a sealed package, if the package was opened after delivery;
  - pkt 10: newspapers, periodicals and magazines, except subscriptions.

Which categories are affected (my reading, not legal advice):

- **Beauty and cosmetics, health and dietary supplements:** pkt 5 once a hygiene seal is broken. Pkt 4 may apply to short-dated products.
- **Books and media:** pkt 9 for sealed CDs, DVDs, games and software once opened. Pkt 10 for single issues of magazines.
- **Jewelry and watches:** pkt 3 for engraved or made-to-order pieces. Pkt 5 may apply to earrings sold sealed for hygiene.
- **Hobby and craft:** pkt 3 for fabric or materials cut or mixed to the buyer's order.
- **Furniture:** pkt 3 for made-to-measure pieces.
- **Car parts and household chemicals:** pkt 6 can apply to fluids once mixed or poured in. Pkt 5 is unlikely.
- **Toys and baby products:** pkt 5 for sealed items like pacifiers or bottle teats.
- **Pet supplies:** pkt 4 for fresh or short-dated food.

EU Directive 2023/2673 requires an online "withdraw from contract" button from 19 June 2026. I did not find a withdrawal-button provision in the 2026 consolidated text, and I did not verify whether Poland has transposed it elsewhere. Treat this as open.

## Return rate sources

Only named studies with a stated method are used. Definitions differ between them, so do not compare them directly.

- **Poland, all categories (seller survey).** [Alsendo, "Zwroty w polskim e-commerce 2026"](https://alsendo.com/app/uploads/2026/04/Alsendo-Raport-Zwroty-w-polskim-e-commerce-koniec-dnia-13.04.2026.pdf). This is a CAWI survey by SW Research of 300 Polish online sellers, run in January and February 2026. It found an average declared return level of 3.5% of products sold, with most firms at or below 5 to 7%. One third of firms do not know their own return rate. The most common handling cost per return was 10 to 15 PLN, and 77% of firms put it between 7 and 30 PLN. 46% of shops make the customer pay for the return and about 33% offer free returns. There is no split by category.
- **Poland and EU, clothing and footwear.** [ETC CE Report 2024/4](https://www.eionet.europa.eu/etcs/etc-ce/products/etc-ce-report-2024-4-volumes-and-destruction-of-returned-and-unsold-textiles-in-europes-circular-economy/@@download/file/Textiles%20destruction%20-%20final%20for%20website%20(2).pdf) by Duhoux, Lingås and Mortensen for the European Environment Agency, summarised in the [EEA briefing](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/the-destruction-of-returned-and-unsold-textiles-in-europes-circular-economy). It is a literature review. It gives an online clothing return rate of 16% for Poland and 20% for the EU average. Footwear ranges from 22 to 37%, with an EU average of 30%.
- **EU, electronics, tools and household items.** [JRC, "Study on the destruction of unsold products" (JRC143466, 2026)](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC143466). It says online return rates for electrical and electronic equipment are "around 5%" for most product categories and uses 5% in its model. It also reports data VITO scraped from the Swiss platform Galaxus: wearables 1.40%, smartphones and tablets 4.97%, manual labour tools 1.34%, kitchen 1.82%. The Galaxus figures are Swiss, not EU or Polish.
- **Cross-border from Poland, beauty.** [Global24 blog, Q4 2024](https://global24.com/pl/blog/zwroty-zagraniczne-w-podziale-na-kraje-i-branze-ivq-2024/). This is company data from Polish shops shipping to seven Western European countries. Beauty had a 2% return rate, stable through 2024. Global24's other category numbers (fashion, electronics, home furnishings, automotive) add up to roughly 100%, so they look like shares of all returns, not return rates. I have not used them as rates. Global24's definition of a return also includes parcels that were never collected and parcels with a wrong address.

## Categories

Each section states my assumed parcel and order value, then the cheapest fitting public option (gross, no contract). Payment fees are given as card / BLIK / Przelewy24. "Shipping plus fee" uses the card fee. Margin outlook is always my judgement, not a sourced fact.

### Clothing and footwear

- **Assumption:** 180 PLN order. A garment in a mailer (35 x 25 x 5 cm, 0.8 kg) or a shoe box (35 x 22 x 13 cm, 1.2 kg).
- **Cheapest option:** ORLEN Paczka S at 13.49 PLN for the mailer, or M at 14.49 PLN for the shoe box.
- **Return rate:** 16% for clothing in Poland, 20% EU average. About 30% for footwear (EU). Source: ETC CE / EEA.
- **Payment fee:** 3.70 / 3.88 / 4.42 PLN.
- **Margin outlook (judgement): thin to medium.** Shipping plus fee is about 17 to 18 PLN (10% of the order). With one in five or six orders coming back, you also lose the refunded outbound shipping, the payment fee, handling (10 to 15 PLN per Alsendo) and often the return label if you offer free returns.

### Home and garden

- **Assumption:** 150 PLN order. A box of garden tools or pots, 45 x 30 x 25 cm, 3 kg.
- **Cheapest option:** ORLEN Paczka L at 15.49 PLN.
- **Return rate:** no published source found. The closest proxy is Galaxus kitchen at 1.82% (Swiss, from the JRC study).
- **Payment fee:** 3.25 / 3.40 / 3.85 PLN.
- **Margin outlook (judgement): medium.** Shipping plus fee is about 19 PLN (13%). Bulky, low-price items like soil or large pots can go over the locker limits and push you onto courier prices.

### Furniture and home decor

- **Assumption, decor:** 200 PLN order, 40 x 35 x 30 cm, 3 kg. **Assumption, flat-pack furniture:** 900 PLN order, 120 x 60 x 15 cm, 30 kg.
- **Cheapest option, decor:** ORLEN Paczka L at 15.49 PLN.
- **Cheapest option, furniture:** It does not fit any locker. DPD courier to courier at 20 to 31.5 kg costs 44.99 PLN gross (DPD point to courier is 35.99 PLN if you drop it off yourself). Check DPD's rules on whether a 120 cm pack counts as non-standard (+29.90 PLN). Pocztex 2XL also fits (sum of sides 195 cm, longest 120 cm) at 55.00 PLN gross. InPost Kurier Standard for business costs 59.08 PLN net plus 13% fuel, which is about 66.76 PLN net. Anything heavy or assembled needs a pallet: InPost from 297.85 PLN net plus fuel, DPD Classic from 195.00 PLN net plus fuel. Public prices exist for all of these.
- **Return rate:** no published source found as a rate. Global24 shows home furnishings as the largest share of returns on the Poland to UK route, but that is a share, not a rate.
- **Payment fee:** decor 4.00 / 4.20 / 4.80 PLN. Furniture 14.50 / 15.40 / 18.10 PLN.
- **Margin outlook (judgement): medium for decor, thin for bulky furniture.** A furniture return can cost as much as the outbound courier or pallet. Art. 12 ust. 1 pkt 10 requires you to state that cost up front, and if you don't, art. 34 ust. 2 puts it on you.

### Consumer electronics accessories

- **Assumption:** 80 PLN order. A cable or case, 20 x 15 x 4 cm, 0.3 kg.
- **Cheapest option:** InPost XS at 11.49 PLN if it is 4 cm or thinner (sent from the InPost Mobile app). Otherwise DPD Pickup at 12.99 PLN (up to 0.5 kg).
- **Return rate:** about 5% for electronics in the EU (JRC). Wearables 1.40% and smartphones and tablets 4.97% (Galaxus, Swiss).
- **Payment fee:** 2.20 / 2.28 / 2.52 PLN.
- **Margin outlook (judgement): medium.** Shipping plus fee is 14 to 15 PLN, which is 17 to 19% of an 80 PLN order. Accessories usually carry high gross margins, which is what saves this category. Cheap orders under 50 PLN get thin fast.

### Beauty and cosmetics

- **Assumption:** 120 PLN order. Two or three products, 20 x 15 x 10 cm, 0.6 kg.
- **Cheapest option:** DPD Pickup at 13.99 PLN (0.5 to 1 kg). ORLEN M is 14.49 PLN.

### Toys and baby products

## Assumptions and gaps

- Parcel sizes, weights and order values are my assumptions for a "typical" order, not sourced figures. Change them and the cheapest carrier can change, because ORLEN and InPost price by size and DPD prices by weight.
- Shipping costs are gross retail prices. A VAT payer really pays about 19% less (gross divided by 1.23), if the carrier issues a VAT invoice. ORLEN's rules mention KSeF e-invoices. I did not check invoicing for InPost's retail channel.
- InPost's retail page does not show the date the current prices started. The 1 March 2026 date comes from press coverage, not from InPost's page.
- DPD's individual page has no version date. DPD's fuel surcharge on business prices is published separately and I did not read it.
- Negotiated prices, broker platforms and Allegro rates (for example Allegro Paczkomat InPost at 8.90 PLN net) are lower, but they are out of scope because they need a contract or a marketplace account.
- Stripe fees are standard pricing. Tax on Stripe fees and Stripe's currency conversion charges were not checked.
- The margin outlook ignores product cost, packaging, marketplace fees and customer acquisition. It only asks how much of the order value shipping, payment and returns take.
- Return rates are missing for 10 of the 15 categories. The sources that do exist use different definitions: survey answers from sellers (Alsendo), literature averages (EEA), and Swiss platform data or cross-border parcels (JRC, Global24).
- Whether Poland has transposed the EU withdrawal button (Directive 2023/2673) is not verified.
